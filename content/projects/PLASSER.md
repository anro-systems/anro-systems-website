# Tolhurst CAD Work Report

## Project Overview
Tolhurst provided advanced CAD services for a leading Australian rail systems manufacturer, delivering precise and detailed 3D models of rail maintenance machinery. The project required integrating mechanical, hydraulic, and structural components into cohesive assemblies that could be used for engineering analysis, technical documentation, and downstream manufacturing. CAD deliverables included both fully assembled models and exploded views to clearly illustrate component relationships and support assembly procedures.

![Exploded View of Rail Maintenance Machine](static/images/projects/PLASSER/p1.png)
*Figure 1: Exploded view of the rail maintenance machine showing key assemblies and structural components.*

## Objectives
The primary goal of the project was to ensure that all designs were accurate, manufacturable, and easy to interpret. CAD models needed to support multiple downstream workflows, including technical manuals, assembly guides, and finite element analysis. Clarity and readability of illustrations were a key requirement, particularly for exploded views and schematics intended for engineers and maintenance teams. Additionally, the CAD work needed to be adaptable for future revisions without compromising design integrity.

![Schematic Illustration of Hydraulic System](p2.png)
*Figure 2: Schematic illustration of the hydraulic system integrated within the machine.*

## Deliverables
Tolhurst produced complete parametric CAD assemblies, including all major mechanical, hydraulic, and structural components. Exploded views and schematic illustrations were created to support technical documentation, and labels were applied to highlight critical components. All CAD files were delivered in formats compatible with industry-standard software, ensuring ease of collaboration and further engineering work. The models were designed to balance colour-coding for clarity, with roughly 30–50% of parts in neutral grey to prevent visual clutter.

## Scope and Notes
The project scope encompassed full assembly design for the maintenance machinery, including sub-assemblies and critical components. Each component was carefully modelled for accuracy and manufacturability. Exploded views were crafted to clearly communicate assembly sequences and component relationships. All proprietary details were anonymised where necessary in shared visuals. The CAD work was delivered with version-controlled files to ensure integrity and collaboration across multiple stakeholders.

![Integrated Assembly View](p4.jpg)
*Figure 4: Integrated assembly view combining mechanical, hydraulic, and structural systems, with balanced colour usage.*

## Technology Used
The CAD work was executed using SolidWorks, AutoCAD, and CATIA for 3D modelling and assembly design. Illustrations and exploded views were enhanced with rendering tools and Adobe Illustrator for technical documentation. All files were managed using a Product Data Management (PDM) system to maintain version control and facilitate collaboration between internal and client teams.

## Stakeholders
The primary client was a leading Australian rail systems manufacturer, with internal stakeholders including CAD engineers, mechanical and hydraulic design teams, and maintenance engineers. External stakeholders, such as manufacturing and operations teams, relied on the CAD deliverables for assembly guidance and maintenance planning.

## Challenges and Complexity
The project required careful integration of complex mechanical, hydraulic, and structural systems into a single cohesive model. Creating exploded views that were both accurate and visually clear demanded careful component selection and colour management. All assemblies were parametric to allow future modifications and finite element analysis. Maintaining confidentiality for client-specific details added an additional layer of complexity, but the final deliverables met all technical requirements and provided significant value for engineering and manufacturing workflows.
